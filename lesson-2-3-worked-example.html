Writing about significance — T·E·E·L

Watch → read → discuss → watch one done → do your own. The sources are on the left; the paragraph is built from them.

The move: T · E · E · L

T — Topic
Your claim, in one sentence. Answers the question directly.
E — Evidence
A quote or a number from a source. Say who said it.
E — Explain
What the evidence means. The “so what”.
L — Link
Tie it back to the question — significance, value, connection.

The question both paragraphs answer: “Explain the significance of this water place to its Traditional Owners, and who decides what happens to it.”

Case study 2 — Budj Bim, Gunditjmara Country

Read the sources below, then write your own TEEL paragraph in OneNote answering the same question. Work on your own.

Your sources

The place On Gunditjmara Country in south-western Victoria, at Tae Rak (Lake Condah). Over thousands of years Gunditjmara People built a vast system of channels and weirs to trap and farm kooyong (eels), and lived in villages of stone houses — evidence of permanent settlement and aquaculture.
What colonisation did After the British took the land, Gunditjmara People fought for Country but were defeated. Homes were destroyed and the aquaculture channels were diverted and damaged by colonists' farms.
Winning the right to decide In 2008, after a long court case, Gunditjmara People gained native title over Country. In 2019 the Budj Bim Cultural Landscape was given UNESCO World Heritage listing. Southern Rural Water reallocated water to Budj Bim.
What that changed Gunditjmara People are now re-establishing the aquaculture system and the kooyong farming and smoking practices for future generations, and the site is visited by people from around the world.

Sources: Australians Together, Values of Water (2023); National Museum of Australia, “Budj Bim Cultural Landscape”; Gunditj Mirring Traditional Owners Aboriginal Corporation.

Your task

Write one TEEL paragraph: “Explain the significance of Budj Bim to Gunditjmara People, and who decides what happens to the water.”

How good is it? (mark your own)

Level 6–7 · identifyI named who is connected to Budj Bim and one way water is used or valued.
Level 8 · describe — our targetI described the significance using two pieces of evidence (a date, a number or a detail) and explained what it means.
Level 9–10 · explainI explained how the answer to “who decides” changed over time (colonisation → native title 2008 → water returned), and compared it with the Martuwarra.

💡 The comparison to aim for: both communities value water deeply — so why does one now control it and the other is still fighting? The difference is whether the law recognises their authorityNative title was only possible after the High Court's Mabo decision (1992) overturned terra nullius. Even now, First Nations Peoples hold under 1% of Australia's water entitlements, despite native title covering roughly 40% of the land..
